\begin{abstract}
\noindent
This manuscript analyzes a single, deterministic evaluation run of the
\textbf{ResearchCollision} harness over twelve purpose-selected, real paired
cross-domain research cases (\texttt{real\_case\_study\_v1}). For each of the
twelve case pairs, four evidence-generation systems were executed:
\texttt{keyword}, \texttt{embedding}, \texttt{llm\_only}, and \texttt{pipeline}
(a keyword+embedding+LLM cascade). Each system was asked to look for evidence on
three ``surfaces'' of the potential research collision between the two fields:
\textbf{intersection} (existing bridging work), \textbf{hypothesis} (motivating
new work), and \textbf{gap} (unanswered bridging space).

The authoritative artifact is \texttt{results.json}, which contains \textbf{188
evidence records} (55 intersection / 50 hypothesis / 83 gap; system totals:
keyword 41, embedding 36, llm\_only 53, pipeline 58). This manuscript is a
\textbf{descriptive, evidence-count report}. Because the evaluation is a single
run with \textbf{no human/expert rating layer}, this paper explicitly makes
\textbf{no claim of evidence quality, relevance, correctness, statistical
significance, or superiority of any system}. Pipeline evidence is incomplete
(coverage 8/12): \textbf{4 pipeline runs failed} (verbatim in Table~D and
Appendix~A) and are reported without repair. All numbers are traceable to
\texttt{results.json} cell-by-cell; the full claim-by-claim verification is in
\texttt{AUDIT.md}.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction and Background}
\label{sec:intro}

\subsection{The research-collision problem}
The ResearchCollision project studies pairwise ``collisions'' between
specialized research fields: given two fields $A$ and $B$ that are each
internally active but poorly connected, harness-based evidence systems attempt to
surface evidence of \textit{intersection}, \textit{hypothesis}, and \textit{gap}.
This operationalizes a long-standing theme in information science ---
undiscovered public knowledge and literature-based discovery (LBD), in which a
hypothesis can be formed when two bodies of literature that individually do not
share citations nevertheless contain complementary statements that, when brought
together, motivate a new research direction \citep{swanson1986, swanson1997}.

\subsection{Contributions and claims}
This manuscript reports:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item A full 12-case $\times$ 4-system evidence matrix (Table~A).
    \item Per-system evidence totals (Table~B) and per-surface distribution
    (Table~C).
    \item A verbatim, unrepaired record of the 4 pipeline failures (Table~D).
    \item A method/configuration appendix and the full evidence corpus for
    reproduction (Appendix~A; \texttt{tables/}).
\end{enumerate}
It makes \textbf{no} claim about which system is \emph{better}. The 4 pipeline
failures are disclosed, and pipeline totals (58 records over 8 cases) are not
treated as comparable to the other systems' 12-case totals.

\section{Methodology}
\label{sec:method}

\subsection{Corpus}
The dataset \texttt{real\_case\_study\_v1} contains 12 paired research cases
(real scholarly topics), each defined by its \texttt{case\_id}
(\texttt{tables/table\_a\_casexsystem.csv}). Each case pairs two research areas;
the pairings are recorded in the dataset metadata in \texttt{results.json}.

\subsection{Systems}
Four systems were evaluated per case:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \texttt{keyword}: lexical keyword-based retrieval.
    \item \texttt{embedding}: dense-embedding retrieval.
    \item \texttt{llm\_only}: direct LLM reasoning without retrieval.
    \item \texttt{pipeline}: keyword + embedding + LLM cascade.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Evidence surfaces}
Each evidence record was classified into exactly one surface:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{intersection}: existing bridging evidence,
    \item \textbf{hypothesis}: evidence motivating a new hypothesis,
    \item \textbf{gap}: evidence of an open / unanswered bridge.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Failure policy}
Runs are stop-on-failure: when a (case, system) pair fails at runtime, no
partial evidence for that cell is recorded. Failures are captured verbatim in
\texttt{results.json} and are reported here \textbf{verbatim} (Table~D,
Appendix~A). Failed cells are \textbf{absent evidence}, not zero evidence.

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:disc}
The observation that gap-type evidence (83/188, 44\%) is the most frequent
surface is consistent with the design of a discovery harness whose goal is to
surface open bridges: for any pair of fields without an established connection,
``gap'' statements are both cheaper and more abundant than verified
intersections. However, this is a count observation on a single run and does not
imply that gap evidence is \emph{valuable} --- value assessment would require a
human rating layer, which is absent in this run.

The pipeline system produced the most records (58) but over only 8 cases; its
totals are therefore \textbf{not comparable} with the other systems' 12-case
totals versions. The 4 pipeline failures (Table~D) disclose an incomplete
pipeline; we do not rank systems.

\section{Threats to Validity}
\label{sec:threats}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Single run, no variance:} no confidence intervals or tests;
    no statistical significance is claimed.
    \item \textbf{No human rating layer:} evidence \emph{quality/relevance/
    correctness} is not assessed; the manuscript does not claim it.
    \item \textbf{Incomplete pipeline coverage:} 4 of 12 pipeline cells failed;
    pipeline totals (Table~B) are reported with that caveat and are not used for
    cross-system ranking.
    \item \textbf{Evidence counts, not findings:} an evidence record attests
    that a system produced a candidate statement on a surface; it does not
    attest correctness.
    \item \textbf{Single deterministic run:} all variance-free numbers come from
    exactly one execution (seed 0); re-runs may differ on LLM quality, network,
    and tool availability.
\end{itemize}
